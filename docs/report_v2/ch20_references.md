# Chapter 20 — References

---

The following sources informed the design, the technology choices, and the engineering practices described in this report. They are grouped by the aspect of the work they bear on.

## Software Engineering Process and Practice

1. Sommerville, I. *Software Engineering.* Pearson. — The principal text underlying the course: process models, requirements engineering, system modelling, architectural design, testing, evolution and reuse.
2. Ghezzi, C., Jazayeri, M., and Mandrioli, D. *Fundamentals of Software Engineering.* Prentice Hall. — Software qualities and the seven engineering principles applied in Chapters 2 and 3.
3. Pressman, R. S., and Maxim, B. R. *Software Engineering: A Practitioner's Approach.* McGraw-Hill.
4. Beck, K., et al. (2001). *Manifesto for Agile Software Development.* agilemanifesto.org.
5. Schwaber, K., and Sutherland, J. *The Scrum Guide.* scrumguides.org. — Roles, ceremonies and the sprint cycle adapted in Section 4.4.4.
6. Beck, K. *Extreme Programming Explained: Embrace Change.* Addison-Wesley. — Test-first development and refactoring, adopted as retrospective actions.
7. Boehm, B. (1988). *A Spiral Model of Software Development and Enhancement.* IEEE Computer. — The risk-driven model surveyed in Section 4.3.4.
8. Kruchten, P. (1995). *Architectural Blueprints — The 4+1 View Model of Software Architecture.* IEEE Software. — The view model applied in Section 9.2.
9. Conway, M. E. (1968). *How Do Committees Invent?* Datamation. — Conway's law, discussed in Section 9.1.
10. Lehman, M. M., and Belady, L. A. *Program Evolution: Processes of Software Change.* Academic Press. — The laws of software evolution applied in Section 14.2.
11. Fowler, M. *Refactoring: Improving the Design of Existing Code.* Addison-Wesley; and *The Practical Test Pyramid*, martinfowler.com.
12. Gamma, E., Helm, R., Johnson, R., and Vlissides, J. *Design Patterns: Elements of Reusable Object-Oriented Software.* Addison-Wesley. — The design patterns catalogued in Table 9.8.

## Modelling and Notation

13. Object Management Group. *Unified Modeling Language (UML) Specification.* omg.org.
14. Rumbaugh, J., Jacobson, I., and Booch, G. *The Unified Modeling Language Reference Manual.* Addison-Wesley.
15. *PlantUML Language Reference Guide.* plantuml.com. — The text notation from which all ten UML diagrams in Chapter 8 are rendered.

16. Pallets Projects. *Flask Documentation.* flask.palletsprojects.com.
17. *SQLAlchemy Documentation.* sqlalchemy.org.
18. *pytest Documentation.* docs.pytest.org.
19. *ReportLab User Guide.* reportlab.com.
20. Pedregosa, F., et al. (2011). *scikit-learn: Machine Learning in Python.* Journal of Machine Learning Research, 12, 2825–2830.

## Cryptography and Security

21. Python Cryptographic Authority. *`cryptography` Library Documentation.* cryptography.io.
22. National Institute of Standards and Technology. *FIPS PUB 180-4: Secure Hash Standard (SHA-256).*
23. Josefsson, S., and Liusvaara, I. (2017). *RFC 8032: Edwards-Curve Digital Signature Algorithm (EdDSA).* IETF. — Ed25519, used for custody-event signatures.
24. National Institute of Standards and Technology. *SP 800-38D: Recommendation for Block Cipher Modes of Operation — Galois/Counter Mode (GCM).* — AES-256-GCM, used for evidence at rest.
25. M'Raihi, D., Machani, S., Pei, M., and Rydell, J. (2011). *RFC 6238: TOTP — Time-Based One-Time Password Algorithm.* IETF.
26. OWASP Foundation. *OWASP Top Ten.* owasp.org. — The threat categories mapped in Table 2.2.
27. OWASP Foundation. *Content Security Policy Cheat Sheet* and *Cross-Site Request Forgery Prevention Cheat Sheet.* cheatsheetseries.owasp.org.

## Machine Learning

28. Liu, F. T., Ting, K. M., and Zhou, Z.-H. (2008). *Isolation Forest.* Proceedings of the 8th IEEE International Conference on Data Mining, 413–422.
29. NVIDIA. *NIM (NVIDIA Inference Microservices) API Documentation.* build.nvidia.com. — The OpenAI-compatible endpoint through which the language model is consumed.

## Domain — Digital Forensics

30. Scientific Working Group on Digital Evidence (SWGDE). *Best Practices for Digital Evidence Collection* and associated chain-of-custody guidance. swgde.org.
31. National Institute of Standards and Technology. *SP 800-86: Guide to Integrating Forensic Techniques into Incident Response.*
32. International Organization for Standardization. *ISO/IEC 27037: Guidelines for identification, collection, acquisition and preservation of digital evidence.*
